The Elements of Professionalism · Part Five

Proactivity

Part One was Integrity. Part Two, High Standards. Part Three, Responsiveness. Part Four, Follow-Through. This one is about doing the work before it becomes urgent — and what that does to how your day feels.

I got on my soapbox about this in our meeting, and I want to put it in writing because I do not think I said it as clearly as I meant to.

Here is the whole idea in one sentence.

Either way, it is work. The only question is when you do it, and how it feels while you are doing it.

That is it. That is the entire argument.

Now let me be honest about the part that makes people avoid it.

Being proactive feels like more work up front — and in that moment, it is. You are doing something today that nothing is forcing you to do today. Nobody is asking for it. Nothing is broken. You could skip it and your afternoon would be easier.

That is real, and pretending otherwise would be silly.

But here is what skipping it actually buys you. You do not avoid the work. You defer it — and it comes back later, bigger, at a worse time, with someone waiting on you and fewer options available. The five minutes you saved turns into an afternoon. The afternoon turns into a week.

You are never choosing between doing the work and not doing it. You are choosing between doing it now, small and quiet, or doing it later, large and loud.

So yes — it is more work today. It is considerably less work overall, and the difference is not close.

What it actually does to you

This is the part I care most about, and it is the part that is hardest to explain to someone who has not felt the difference.

Reactive work and proactive work can look identical from the outside. Same task, same phone call, same email. But they feel nothing alike, and the difference is not small.

The same taskDone earlyDone under pressure
Who set the timingYou didSomeone else did
How many options you haveSeveralUsually one
Who is waiting on youNobodyAn owner, a tenant, or both
Room to think it throughYesNo
Cost if you get it wrongSmall, and fixableLarge, and visible
How it feelsOrdinaryExhausting

Working reactively means you are always performing. Someone is watching, something is already broken, and you are being judged on how well you handle a situation you did not choose. That is a genuinely draining way to spend a day, and it adds up over a week in a way people underestimate.

Working proactively is quiet. Nobody is watching. You are moving through your own list at your own pace, and the things you handle never become anything anyone else has to know about.

One of those preserves your sanity. The other spends it.

And at the end of the week you have done a comparable amount of work either way. You just paid for it very differently.

The quiet part

There is something else in this that I think is worth naming.

When you work ahead, you are controlling the narrative. Not in a manipulative sense — in the sense that you decide what the story of the week is rather than having it decided for you.

You choose which conversations happen and when. You bring the owner information before they ask for it, so the exchange is you informing them rather than you explaining yourself. You reach the vendor before you need them urgently, so you are a good customer rather than an emergency.

Reactive work puts you permanently on the back foot, answering for things. Proactive work puts you in front, setting the terms.

The work is quiet, nobody applauds it, and it is the entire difference between a job that wears you down and one you can do for years.

So which do you prefer?

None of this is about working more. I am not asking anyone for extra hours. I am asking for the same hours, spent earlier, on your terms rather than someone else's.

If you value peace, you will value proactivity and attention to detail. If you stay in reactive mode, the stress comes with it. Those two things travel together and they always have.

So which do you prefer?

You answer that question every week, in what you choose to handle before it becomes urgent.

Thank you, all of you, for the way you show up.
